\begin{abstract}
Robot foundation models, from vision-language-action models to world action models, are pre-trained on web-scale video and cross-embodiment robot data and then post-trained on demonstrations of a target robot and environment.
This recipe works well when hundreds of demonstrations are available, but we show that it degrades sharply when only a few demonstrations per task can be collected: post-training the pre-trained \base model on three demonstrations per task reduces its LIBERO success rates from 87--99\% to 58--91\%.
We argue that pre-training on a multi-embodiment mixture is not the same as learning to \emph{adapt} from few demonstrations, and propose \Method, a stage inserted between pre-training and post-training.
Using robot datasets that are already part of the pre-training mixture, the stage repeatedly simulates few-shot post-training---a short fine-tuning on a few demonstrations of one source embodiment---and moves the weights toward the adapted solution with a first-order interpolation update.
Because the update is a convex combination of weights, the entire trainable set of the post-training recipe can be meta-trained with the standard sharded training stack, and the result is a drop-in replacement for the pre-trained weights.
We further observe that few-shot post-training over-relies on the visual context and under-uses the instruction, and introduce \rpl, a post-training objective that samples negative instructions and requires the action prediction under a wrong instruction to be worse than under the correct one.
With three demonstrations per task, our approach raises the average success rate over LIBERO-10, -Goal, -Spatial, and -Object from 71.7\% to 78.7\% (58.0/63.2/74.8/90.6\% to 69.4/76.2/78.6/90.4\%) and improves every LIBERO-10 task, whereas ordinary training on the same source data yields no gain (58.2\% on LIBERO-10).
The benefit is not limited to the few-shot regime: with all demonstrations, LIBERO-10 improves from 87.2\% to 94.6\%.
\end{abstract}
